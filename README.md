### Q1: How has female labor force participation evolved since 1994? 
According to the Current Population Survey (CPS) data, the female labor force participation (LFP) for all women has not seen a significant increase from 1994 to 2024 (approximately 59.2%). However, when we take a more granular approach and examine the group of working women (between the age of 25 and 54), we see that the LFP does increase by approximately +3 percentage points, from 75.2% to 78.3%. Overall, there seems to be more women of working age who are participating in the labor force over the last 30 years, with higher flucuations leading up to the year 2000 and a decline as we approach 2015 and 2020.

### Q2: Among women older than 25, which groups (race, age, income percentile, etc.) of people had the biggest changes in labor force participation since 1994?
![Q2 Figure 1](Figures/Part%201/Part_1_q2_Figure_1.png)

Hispanic women and women of 2 or more races seemed to have the greatest increases in LFP in the 30 year span, while Black, Asian, and Native American women saw more moderate increases. Conversely, white women have decreased their participation in the labor force since 1994. 

![Q2 Figure 2](Figures/Part%201/Part_1_q2_Figure_2.png)

25-54 year old women seem to have the highest LFP rates over the 30 year span, with little fluctuation. 55-64 year old women seem to be stayin in the labor force for longer in 2024 than in 1994, with 62% participating in the labor force in 2024 versus 49% in 1994 (13% increase). 65-75 year olds sensibly have low LFP rates, given the aging demographic. 

![Q2 Figure 3](Figures/Part%201/Part_1_q2_Figure_3.png)

Women at the 60th income percentile and above have similarly high LFP rateswith a slight dropoff for women in the 60th to 80th percentile range between 1994 and 2024 (approximately 4 percentage points). Women in the 40th to 60th percentile range have dropped from 77% to 70.5% LFP in the 30 year span. Women in the 20-40th percentile range dropped from 44% to 36% LFP, and women in the lowest percentile range dropped from 26% to 17% LFP. The general trend across most income groups is that LFP seems to be dropping, likely due to these women reaching retirement age as they get older.
